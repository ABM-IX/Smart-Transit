Introduction & Problem Statement

Urban Paratransit Context

Informal and semi-formal paratransit carries 70%–90% of motorized passenger trips in developing African cities (Cervero, 2000). Greater Gaborone relies on three uncoordinated modes: combis (minibuses), standard/special taxis, and intercity buses (Lusenge, 2020).

Problem Statement

Decentralized operation causes severe vehicle bunching (long service gaps followed by clustered arrivals), unpredictable commuter waiting times, arbitrary off-meter taxi fares, and fragmented transfers between suburban origins and arterial corridors.

Gaborone Transit Corridors
Literature Review & Identified Research Gaps
1. Passive Tracking Fallacy Apps display icons on maps but lack active headway spacing control loops (Daganzo, 2009).
2. First/Last-Mile Void Suburban commuters lack coordinated links between on-demand cabs and arterial combi routes.
3. Static GTFS Mismatch Fixed schedules fail in paratransit operating on "depart-when-full" occupant demand.
4. Zero Regulatory Observability DRTS regulators lack live telematics to audit route compliance, fares, and bottleneck patterns.

Conclusion & Academic Contribution

  • Active Coordination over Passive Tracking: SmartTransit demonstrates that African paratransit can achieve service regularity without rigid, brittle timetables by deploying real-time inter-vehicle headway control and spatial dispatch.
  • Turnkey Engineering Contribution: Delivered an event-driven architecture with 6 mathematically formulated algorithms, transparent dual-rate DRTS fare enforcement, and a desktop macro-regulatory telemetry hub.
  • Public Transit Parity: Effectively bridges first/last-mile suburban connectivity by synchronizing local on-demand cabs with scheduled intercity coach services.

Aim & Research Objectives

Project Aim

To design, develop, and evaluate SmartTransit, a real-time multi-modal Intelligent Transportation System (ITS) that actively coordinates Gaborone's taxis, combis, and intercity buses rather than merely displaying them passively.

Targeted Outcomes
Core Engineering Objectives
  • O1. Real-Time Telemetry: Stream 1 Hz vehicle GPS telemetry over persistent WebSockets.
  • O2. Spatial Taxi Dispatch: Match commuters to the 5 nearest special cabs (k=5) within 500 ms.
  • O3. Headway Regulation: Detect corridor bunching and stream in-cab pacing advisories.
  • O4. Automated Geofencing: Detect boarding/alighting via distance and speed co-movement.
  • O5. Multi-Modal Planning: Synthesize taxi-combi-bus routes with backward buffers.
  • O6. Regulatory Telematics: Provide an operational React dashboard with 5 export reports.

Discussion: The algorithms address different failure points in an irregular, demand-led network. Haversine distance and spatial k-NN support nearby-vehicle matching; the headway rule turns GPS updates into pacing advice intended to reduce bunching; and the speed floor keeps ETA estimates finite when traffic slows. Co-movement geofencing can record boarding and alighting without a manual tap, while the multi-modal graph applies a transfer buffer when joining combi and bus legs. The evaluation therefore combines a measured API response (340 ms) with design targets for GPS precision, waiting-time reduction, route feasibility and user acceptance. These targets need validation against a larger field sample before they can be treated as demonstrated service improvements. Together, the results show a technically integrated prototype; they do not yet establish network-wide impact.

Recommendations & Future Work

  • Policy Recommendations: Deploy in-cab pacing tablets with local Combi Associations; utilize fleet telematic logs for municipal route restructuring and congestion pricing.
  • Study Limitations: 12-week prototype timeframe; geographic focus on Greater Gaborone; pilot UAT limited to 10–15 drivers and commuters; reliance on 3G/4G cellular coverage.
  • Future Directions: Integrate Orange Money / SmarTeam cashless payments; develop BLE offline mesh sync for dead-zones; scale graph routing across SADC corridors.
